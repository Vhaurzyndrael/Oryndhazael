# Oryndhazael

> A bell of green glass rings only inside the silence of a sleeping mountain.

## The Bell Without a Tower

The bell hangs from a strand of silver hair in a room beneath the mountain. No road reaches the room. There is no entrance on the slope. Visitors find it by walking until their shadows stop following, then waiting for the shadows to return carrying a little dust.

The bell is made of green glass. Within its walls, narrow lines resemble the veins of a leaf. Its clapper is absent. When touched, the glass produces a warmth that moves through the fingertips and settles behind the eyes.

The room contains a stool, a basin, and a coat suspended from a nail. The coat is always damp at the shoulders. Nothing else is wet. On the stool lies a glove with seven fingers, all carefully folded inward.

Nobody has claimed these belongings. Nobody has tried them on twice.

## The Listener's Journey

One visitor was a woman who had lost the sound of her own footsteps. She could hear the steps of others, the scrape of furniture, and the fall of rain. Her own walking remained silent, even on broken glass.

Her shadow returned with dust from the room. She followed it beneath the mountain and sat on the stool. The glove unfolded beside her. Seven fingers pointed toward seven places on the bell's surface.

She touched the lowest place first. A distant road appeared in the basin. On the road, a child walked beside a tall figure wearing the damp coat. She recognized the child and could not recognize the figure. The child carried a stone wrapped in a ribbon.

The woman touched another place. The basin showed the same road in winter. The child was gone. The coat hung from a tree, its sleeves moving though the branches remained still.

## Seven Places of Warmth

Each place on the bell holds a different warmth. One is the warmth of a room after its occupants have departed. Another belongs to a hand held beneath a wing. A third is the warmth inside a name before anyone has spoken it.

The remaining four have been described in contradictory ways. One visitor called them seasons. Another called them sisters. A third insisted that they were all the same warmth, approached from four directions that could not exist together.

When all seven places are touched at once, the silver hair grows taut. The mountain shifts by an amount too small to measure, and too large to overlook. Far above, stones move uphill. Streams pause at the edges of cliffs. Birds land facing the inside of their own wings.

The bell makes no audible sound. Nevertheless, anyone asleep nearby dreams of a room in which somebody has just stopped singing.

## Beneath the Basin

The basin stands on three feet shaped like closed claws. Beneath it lies a round patch of floor that never gathers dust. Shadows cross this patch reluctantly. The visitor's shadow refused to cross it at all.

Looking into the basin from directly above reveals a dark eye beneath the water. The eye remains closed. Around it, overlapping scales form a landscape of ridges and valleys. In the deepest valley stands another green bell, smaller than a seed.

The woman leaned closer. A wing moved beneath the water without making a ripple. The basin widened until it resembled a lake, and the room stretched around it like cloth drawn over a sleeping body.

She stepped back. Everything returned to its former size. The glove now lay open on the stool, and one of its fingers held the ribbon from her childhood stone.

## The Unheard Inscription

There is writing on the inside of the bell. It appears as bubbles in the glass and changes whenever the mountain receives snow. The most persistent arrangement has been transcribed as:

*Oryn — dha — zael — vesh.*

Reading the words aloud produces no echo. Reading them silently causes the coat to drip once into the basin. Each drop contains a tiny image of a door opening beneath a different sky.

The woman read the inscription seven times. Seven drops fell. Six showed doors. The seventh showed a pair of feet standing on the distant road, with no body above them and no shadow below.

She reached into the water. It felt dry. Something placed a small stone in her palm, wrapped in a ribbon that was still warm from the glove.

## A Sound Returned Sideways

The woman left the room before her shadow was ready. On the slope, the sun appeared lower than the mountain allowed. Behind her came a single footstep, then another. She turned and saw only the familiar outline of her shadow, walking several paces late.

Her own footsteps had returned. They sounded like someone moving through snow in a room with a wooden floor. Sometimes they continued after she stopped. Sometimes they arrived before she began.

She kept the stone on her windowsill. Its ribbon loosened every winter and tightened every spring. When the mountain was hidden by clouds, the stone became transparent, revealing a tiny coat folded inside.

The room remains beneath the mountain.

The bell remains suspended.

And the silver hair, which has never broken, grows slightly longer whenever somebody mistakes silence for an empty place.
